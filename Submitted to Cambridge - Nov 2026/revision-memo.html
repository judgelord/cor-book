1 Revisions to Interbranch Representation in response to book conference feedback

Background: In July 2026, we held a day-long book conference at the University of Michigan. Participants included: David Lewis (Vanderbilt), Sharece Thrower (Vanderbilt), Molly Reynolds (Brookings), Justin Grimmer (Stanford), Laurel Harbridge (Northwestern), Kenneth Lowande (University of Michigan/Rice University), Charles Shipan (University of Michigan), and Donald Moynihan (University of Michigan).

We are so grateful for the incredibly thoughtful and constructive feedback from conference participants. This manuscript is much stronger because of their advice. Below, we summarize our major revisions and what remains.

2 Main changes

  • [Reorganized the manuscript]: At the book workshop, we asked participants about an alternative structure for the book, and the consensus was that this alternative was a better approach. We thus reorganized the book around the three drivers of interbranch representation in our framework rather than types of interbranch representation, which means the five chapters about each type of interbranch representation outcome (constituency service, policy work, etc.) were replaced by chapters organized by independent variables (demand, capacity, incentives). This required adding a chapter about the different types of interbranch representation. We have also separated the general theoretical discussion of our framework from measurement. This facilitates one of the suggestions from the workshop: that we emphasize the theoretical contribution as advancing a broader research program, separating out our empirical contributions as only part of that program. The new chapters 2 and 3 distinguish the causes and effects portion of the framework.

  • Added descriptive detail and examples: Throughout the manuscript, we added anecdotes and examples from the data. This responds to requests for more descriptive details and qualitative characterization of the data.
  • Removed inequality focus: Throughout the manuscript, our primary focus is now on explaining variation in interbranch representation rather than explaining inequality per se. Functionally, this is a matter of emphasis and presentation rather than any substantive change. For example, our regression models are the same, but we have replaced the primary presentation of all results with figures that show estimated percentage change in interbranch representation rather than estimated changes in distributions and Gini coefficients. We now only present estimated changes in distribution when this adds richness beyond the primary relationships we study, i.e., when effects are both statistically significant and uneven across the distribution. This responds to feedback at the book conference that the word “inequality” evoked normative concerns, whereas “variation” did not, even though they are two ways of talking about the same empirical phenomena.

4 Still to come

  • Stylistic Edit from professional book editor: The manuscript is currently receiving a stylistic edit from Kelly Clancy (former tenured political science professor, now head of Epilogue Editing). We anticipate receiving her comments back by February 2027.
  • Interviews with Congressional staff and Members: While the manuscript is under review, we plan to conduct interviews to add richness to the manuscript.
  • Augment our results with District-level models: The current analysis is at the legislator level. While the manuscript is under review, we plan to develop district-level robustness checks of all of our results fully. Our theory generally has similar theoretical predictions if we use the district rather than the legislator as the unit of analysis. We generally see similar results with a district-level analysis, but have not comprehensively tested our hypotheses with district-level models. An added benefit of doing this analysis is that district-level results can augment the results currently in the book, where we are unable to estimate difference-in-differences models due to variables that do not change within a legislator.
