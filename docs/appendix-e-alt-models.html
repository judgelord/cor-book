Appendix E — Alternative Models (Online Only)

E.1 Alternative Specifications

For three types of results, we used modified versions of Equation C.1. We estimate additional models as robustness checks.

Majority Status

Because committee leadership positions depend on chair status, we omitted majority status when estimating chair effects. To estimate the effects of majority status on interbranch work, we added a majority status indicator to Equation C.1. We also estimated modified models with majority status but without committee leadership variables. Each section includes a model where we exclude committee leadership positions that depend on majority status, in order to estimate the effect of majority status among all members (not just those without leadership positions, who are structurally more likely to be in the minority, or junior members of the majority).

PAC Contributions

Because fundraising is also closely tied to committee positions as both a cause of appointment to chair positions and an effect of leadership positions, we did not include PAC fundraising in the main specification to estimate committee leadership effects. To estimate relationships with PAC contributions, we estimated separate models, identical to Equation C.1, but adding PAC contributions.

Right Wing and Left Wing

Our main specification estimates the relationship between political party and interbranch representation, controlling for the absolute value of 1st Dimension DW-NOMINATE scores. To estimate the difference in interbranch representation by the right wing, we estimate modified models where we substitute a “right wing” (NOMINATE scores greater than 0.5) indicator for the party indicator and the absolute value of 1st Dimension DW-NOMINATE scores. Finally, to compare right-wing legislators to moderates, we added a control for “left wing” (NOMINATE scores less than -0.5).

Robustness Checks

The main specification used a combined modeling approach. Here we separate the parts of our framework and test the effects of constituent demand, capacity, and the Incentives on interbranch representation separately, without adjusting for effects of the other parts. This alternative specification may be informative if we believe that parts are causally related (for example, if people from large states are more likely to become committee chairs or if more moderate members are more likely to face competitive general elections), or if some variables may be co-linear.

Modeling Response to Constituency Demands
